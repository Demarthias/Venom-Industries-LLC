Why it matters

Your website is often the first thing a customer sees

Before someone calls a plumber, books a dentist or hires a roofer, they usually look them up on their phone. In a few seconds they decide whether to call you or the next result. These are the problems that quietly lose those customers.

It's too slow on phones

Most local searches happen on a phone, often on a cellular connection. A page that takes several seconds to show anything looks broken, and people hit "back" and pick a competitor.

Google also measures speed through its Core Web Vitals and factors it into which sites it shows first.

Google can't tell what you do

If your pages are titled "Home" with no city or service, Google has to guess. Missing titles, descriptions and headings are some of the most common problems on small business sites, and some of the cheapest to fix.

It's hard to get in touch

A phone number that can't be tapped, no quote form, or no obvious "Get a quote" button all add friction. Every extra step loses some people who were ready to hire you.

It looks untrustworthy

A "Not secure" warning in the address bar, a copyright year from 2019 or links that lead to error pages make a business look closed or careless, even when it isn't.

Some people can't use it

Low-contrast text, unlabeled buttons and images without descriptions make a site hard to use for people with poor eyesight or disabilities, and for anyone reading in bright sunlight.

You can't see what's working

Without analytics or call tracking, you don't know how many people visit or how many call from the site, so you can't tell if your website, Google listing or ads are paying off.

The free audit

What the free website audit checks

The audit uses Google Lighthouse, the same engine behind Google's PageSpeed Insights, plus hands-on checks of the things that turn visitors into calls. It's run on a simulated phone over a mobile connection, because that's how most local customers find you.

Technical checks

  • Page speed. How long your page takes to show something useful on a phone (Google calls this Largest Contentful Paint), and what's slowing it down: oversized photos, too many scripts, no caching.
  • Mobile friendliness. Whether text is readable, buttons are tappable, and the layout fits a phone screen without zooming or sideways scrolling.
  • Search engine basics (SEO). Page titles, descriptions, headings and the other signals Google uses to understand what you do and where you do it.
  • Accessibility. Color contrast, image descriptions, form labels and other issues that make the site hard to use for people with poor eyesight or disabilities.
  • Security. Whether you have a valid security certificate, or browsers are labeling your site "Not secure".
  • Broken links. Links and buttons that lead to error pages or nowhere.
  • Platform. Which website builder or system your site runs on, so the report only suggests fixes that are actually possible on it.

Customer checks

  • Can people call you? Is your phone number a tap-to-call link on mobile, or just text people have to copy?
  • Can people ask for a quote? Is there a contact or quote form that works, or only an email address?
  • Is the next step obvious? Are there clear "Get a quote" or "Book now" buttons where people look for them?
  • Do you know what's working? Is there any analytics or call tracking, or are you guessing how many people visit?
  • Does it look current? Old copyright years, dead social links and outdated info make a business look closed.
  • A phone screenshot. You'll see exactly what a customer sees when they open your site on an iPhone.

What's in the report

  • Your scores. Google's speed, SEO, accessibility and best-practice scores, out of 100, with your page load time on a phone.
  • A phone screenshot of your homepage as a customer sees it.
  • Each problem in plain English: what's wrong, and why it costs you customers.
  • A fixed price for each fix, a total, and how many business days it would take.
  • A rebuild recommendation only if your site truly can't be fixed.

What it doesn't involve

  • No logins. The audit only looks at the public website, the same way a customer does. You don't need to share any passwords.
  • No changes. Nothing on your site is touched until you've approved a quote.
  • No pressure. If your site is in good shape, the report says so. If you don't want any fixes, that's the end of it.
  • No sales calls. You get the report by email. You reach out if you want to go further.

Plain English

Website terms, explained

You'll see some of these in your report. Here's what they actually mean.

Lighthouse / PageSpeed score
Google's free test that grades a page from 0 to 100 on speed, SEO, accessibility and best practices. 90 or above is good; under 50 is poor.
Largest Contentful Paint (LCP)
How long it takes for the main part of the page, usually the big photo or headline, to appear. Google considers under 2.5 seconds good.
Core Web Vitals
Google's three measurements of how a page feels to use: how fast it loads, how quickly it responds to a tap, and whether things jump around while loading.
SEO
Search engine optimization. Making your site easy for Google to understand, so it shows you to people searching for what you do, where you do it.
Meta description
The short summary under your link in Google results. If you don't write one, Google picks a random sentence from the page.
HTTPS / SSL certificate
The padlock in the address bar. It encrypts what visitors send you and stops browsers from labeling your site "Not secure".
Caching
Saving a ready-made copy of your pages so they don't have to be rebuilt for every visitor. One of the biggest speed improvements on WordPress.
Accessibility (WCAG)
Making a site usable for people with disabilities. WCAG is the widely used standard for it, and what most ADA website complaints point to.
Call to action (CTA)
The button or link that tells visitors what to do next, like "Get a quote" or "Call now".
Google Business Profile
Your listing on Google Maps and in local search results. Your website should match its name, address and phone exactly.
